1 Purpose of this note
This note clarifies a possiblerelationalextension of the Feature-Enriched Radiation Model
(FERM). The goal is to keep theradiation mechanismunchanged while extending the way
attractiveness is represented.
The key principle is the following:
The mobility mechanism remains the same as in the Radiation Model and in
the traditional FERM: particles leave an origin, inspect destinations in order
of increasing distance, and are absorbed by the first destination whose offer
exceeds their threshold. What changes across model variants is only the way
the latent threshold and offer distributions are centered.
The main motivation is that in many empirical settings attractiveness is not only destination-
specific, but alsocorridor-specific: the same destination may be more attractive from
one origin than from another because of language similarity, visa openness, diplomatic
relations, diaspora networks, historical ties or other bilateral factors.

5 Radiation Model
The Radiation Model (RM) is recovered as the baseline case in which attractiveness is
completely neutral. In terms of the common matrix formulation, this means
Σij = 0 for alli, j.(4)
Therefore both the threshold and the offer distributions are centered at zero:

1,...,mi

1,...,nj
N(0, σ 2).
In this case, no exogenous attractiveness term is introduced. The model is driven only by:
•the population of the originm i,
•the population of the destinationn j,
•the sequential ordering of destinations by distance.

8 Combined model
A more general model may contain both node-level and corridor-level information. In the
present note we donotcommit to a unique composition rule such as
Σij =µ j +δ ij,
because this may or may not be the appropriate specification.
Instead, we define a combined model more generally as any specification in which:
•the diagonal preserves the origin baseline,
Σii =µ i,
• the off-diagonal entries Σ ij for i̸ = j encode both destination-level and corridor-level
information through a chosen composition rule.
This rule could be additive, multiplicative or another transformation depending on the
empirical application and interpretation of the features.

10 Interpretation ofΣ ij
The sign of Σ ij should be interpretable:
•Σ ij >0: corridor (i, j) is more attractive than neutral,
•Σ ij = 0: neutral corridor,
•Σ ij <0: corridor (i, j) is more repulsive than neutral.
Examples of attractive bilateral signals include:
•social connectedness,
•common language or religion similarity,
•visa openness,
•diaspora networks,
•strong trade ties.
Examples of repulsive bilateral signals include:
•diplomatic disagreement,
•political tension,
•closed borders,
•historical hostility,
•large generalized migration frictions.

11 Normalization and renormalization issues
This section is crucial. Bilateral features cannot be inserted directly into Σ ij without
transformation.
11.1 Why normalization is needed
Raw bilateral features differ in:
•units of measurement,
•numerical range,
•skewness,
•sign interpretation,
•missing-data pattern.
For example, trade flows can span many orders of magnitude, while religious similarity may
already lie in [0 , 1]. Without normalization, the Gaussian means would not be comparable
across different empirical choices ofδ ij.
11.2 Sign orientation
Some features are attractive when large, others are repulsive when large. Therefore each
empirical bilateral featureδ raw
ij should first be oriented into a signed score:
δoriented

−δraw

This ensures that the sign of Σ ij has a consistent interpretation.
11.3 Centering
If we define
Σij >0 attractive,Σ ij = 0 neutral,Σ ij <0 repulsive,
then transformations that map values to [0 , 1] are conceptually problematic, because zero
no longer means neutral.
For a relational FERM with meaningful attraction/repulsion semantics, centering around
zero is preferable. Suitable choices include:
•z-score normalization,
•affine scaling to [−1,1],
•centered rank transforms.

11.4 Heavy-tailed variables
Some bilateral features, such as trade or social connectedness, are highly right-skewed. For
such variables a useful pipeline is:
1. apply a monotone compression such as log(1 +x),
2. orient the sign,
3. center and scale.
This prevents a few extreme corridors from dominating the Gaussian mean shifts.
11.5 Missing values
Missing bilateral information is not trivial. Replacing missing values by zero implicitly
assumes that the corridor is neutral. Replacing them by the minimum observed value
assumes that the corridor is maximally unattractive. Both are substantive modeling
assumptions.
Therefore missing-value treatment should be discussed explicitly, because it affects the
meaning of Σ ij.
11.6 Split comparability
Normalization should be performed consistently across validation and test sets. If the
transformation is estimated separately on each split, the same raw corridor may receive
different normalized values in different samples. This makes model comparison difficult.
A cleaner procedure is:
•fit the normalization rule on a fixed reference sample,
•apply the same transformation unchanged to all splits.
11.7 Renormalization in the combined model
The combined model raises an additional issue: destination-level and corridor-level features
may live on different scales. If they are combined into a single Σij, one must decide whether
the combination happens:
•before normalization,
•after separate normalization,
•after separate weighting,
•or through a different joint transformation.
This is one reason why the present note does not impose a unique composition rule for the
combined variant.

13 Takeaway
The main contribution of this formulation is conceptual clarity:
1. The radiation mechanism remains unchanged.
2. The model family is unified through a single attractiveness matrixΣ.
3. The diagonal entries Σ ii determine threshold centers.
4. The off-diagonal entries Σ ij determine corridor-specific offer centers.
5. The main unresolved issue in practice is not the mechanism itself, but the empirical
construction and normalization of Σ ij.
In this sense, the relational extension is not a different mobility process, but a different
parameterization of attractiveness within the same radiation-style absorption framework.
